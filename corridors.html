Five regions. Every direction. One mesh.

This is not hub-and-spoke, and Canada is not the hub. Any business in any of the five regions can sign up and trade with any other region: export to it, and import from it. Five regions, each shipping to the other four, is 20 directed corridors, all on the same classification, document, freight and escrow rails. The product is a mesh from the architecture. The rollout is still sequenced (see below), but every region that opens unlocks both directions at once.

Directed corridors, one set of rails

5 origins × 4 destinations. Every corridor gets its own compliance pack (treaty, duty, labelling, and biosecurity where AU/NZ is involved), but the workflow never changes: classify, price the landed cost, generate documents, book freight, fund escrow, release on clearance.

Examples: Australia → US · Europe → US · US → Australia · NZ → Europe · Europe → Canada · Canada → NZ.

Sequencing, honestly: the mesh is the architecture, not the launch plan. Regions open in sequence (Canada-US first as the validation wedge, then Europe, then AU/NZ as Phase 3) because each new region needs broker coverage, compliance data and, for AU/NZ, biosecurity packs. But an account is never locked to one origin or one destination: as each region opens, it opens in both directions, and one account ends up covering up to four destination markets. AU/NZ remain attractive as Phase 3 for the same reasons as before: English-speaking, similar legal systems, high-value food, wine, beauty and agtech, and the strictest biosecurity rules in the network.
